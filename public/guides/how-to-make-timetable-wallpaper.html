How to make a timetable wallpaper for your phone

A clean timetable on your lock screen does more than look good — it removes the friction of checking "what's next" and quietly keeps you on track.

Why a timetable wallpaper works

Most students keep their schedule in an app they have to open. A wallpaper puts it one glance away, every time you pick up your phone. That tiny reduction in friction is what makes a habit stick: you see your next class before you even unlock.

Step 1: Gather your details

Before designing anything, collect the raw material:

  • Your days of the week (Mon–Fri, or include weekends if you have classes).
  • Your time rows (e.g. 8:00, 9:00, 10:00 …) — only the hours you actually have classes.
  • Each class: name, day, start time, end time, and optionally a color and room.
  • Your phone's screen resolution (see our wallpaper sizes cheat sheet).

Step 2: Pick your screen ratio

The wallpaper must match your device's aspect ratio, otherwise it will be cropped or stretched. Use The Scheduler-inator! landing page to pick your device (iPhone, Android, tablet, or monitor) — the tool sizes the canvas automatically.

Step 3: Build the grid

  1. Add only the time rows you need — fewer rows means a cleaner look.
  2. Select the days you have classes.
  3. Add each class with its time and days. The tool will warn you about overlaps.
  4. Color-code by subject so your eye can scan quickly.

Step 4: Style it

Open Settings to adjust fonts, sizes, and spacing. Tips for an aesthetic result:

  • Keep text small but legible — you are glancing, not reading.
  • Use a dark or muted background so class colors pop.
  • Set a background image that is calm (gradients, soft photos) rather than busy.
  • Leave breathing room — white space is what makes it look premium.

Step 5: Export and set as wallpaper

Hit "Download as Image", then set it as your lock screen wallpaper. On most phones you can choose "Fit to Screen" or "Fill"; match the option to your canvas size.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Too many rows. If a row has no class, remove it.
  • Tiny fonts on large screens. Scale text to your device.
  • Busy backgrounds. They fight with the text.
  • Forgetting safe areas. Notches and home indicators can cover content — preview on your actual device.

Make it a habit

A wallpaper only helps if it stays current. Update it each semester, and whenever your schedule changes. Pair it with a weekly review (Sunday evening works well) so the schedule in your head matches the one on your screen.
