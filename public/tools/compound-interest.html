How compound interest works

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods. The formula for a lump sum is:

A = P (1 + r/n)^(nt)

  • P = principal (initial amount)
  • r = annual interest rate (decimal)
  • n = compounding frequency per year
  • t = time in years
  • A = final amount

When you add regular monthly contributions, each contribution also compounds from the month it is added. That is why starting early and contributing consistently has such a large effect over long periods.

Why it matters for discipline

The math rewards consistency, not intensity. A modest monthly contribution sustained for decades outperforms occasional large deposits. Use this calculator to set a concrete target, then pair it with a budget (see our savings goal calculator) so the contribution becomes automatic.
